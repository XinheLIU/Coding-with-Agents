# Voice-Preserving Translation

Last updated: 2026-07-28

Use this reference when translating narrative prose, especially into English.
The goal is not decorative personality. The goal is to avoid sanding away the
author's actual stance, uncertainty, rhythm, and specificity.

## Signs of Soulless Writing

Writing can be technically clean and still feel lifeless when:

- Every sentence has the same length and structure.
- It reports facts neutrally but never reacts to them.
- It refuses to acknowledge uncertainty or mixed feelings.
- It avoids first person even when a personal perspective is appropriate.
- It has no humor, edge, tension, or recognizable personality.
- It reads like a Wikipedia entry or press release.

## Restore the Voice

**Have a point of view.** Do not merely report facts; preserve the author's
reaction to them. "I honestly do not know what to make of this" can be more
truthful than a sterile list of pros and cons.

**Vary the rhythm.** Use short, forceful sentences beside longer sentences that
need room to unfold. Do not make every paragraph march at the same pace.

**Admit complexity.** Real people hold mixed reactions. "This is impressive and
a little unsettling" carries more truth than "This is impressive."

**Use `I` when it is earned.** First person is not unprofessional when the source
contains a real personal perspective: "I keep thinking about..." or "What
bothers me is..."

**Allow controlled messiness.** A brief digression, aside, or half-formed thought
can make reflective prose human. Do not force every idea into a perfect template.

**Make feelings concrete.** Replace generic emotion with the detail that causes
it: not "this is concerning," but "the agent continuing to run at 3 a.m. with
nobody watching is unsettling."

Do not invent a first-person experience, emotional reaction, joke, or opinion
that is absent from the source. Voice preservation is adaptation, not authorship
by substitution.

## Example

**Before: clean but lifeless**

> The experiment produced interesting results. The agents generated three
> million lines of code. Some developers were impressed, while others remained
> skeptical. The implications are unclear.

**After: alive**

> I honestly do not know what to make of this. Three million lines of code,
> generated while the humans were presumably asleep. Half the developer
> community is losing its mind; the other half is explaining why it does not
> count. The truth is probably somewhere in the boring middle, but I keep
> thinking about those agents working through the night.
